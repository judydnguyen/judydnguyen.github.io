I’m Judy | CS Ph.D. student @ Vanderbilt, advised by Prof. Taylor T. Johnson & Prof. Kevin Leach | SoICT alum | VinUni research alum | Vietnamese blood in me. I was lucky to spend the past two summers at the Center for AI Safety and Microsoft Research.

About myself. Amid the accelerating AI race, AI has made knowledge more accessible and complex ideas easier to grasp. Yet, a question lingers: How much do we truly understand about the LLMs and AI agents we increasingly rely on? As we equip them with more powerful tools and more autonomy, is our ability to understand, oversee, and control their behavior keeping pace?

These questions have shaped my curiosity and research direction. I study the cybersecurity threats and safety risks of AI systems, from prompt injection and privacy leakage to rogue agent behaviors and misalignment, so that progress does not come at the cost of security, safety, and meaningful human control.

Beyond research. I’m passionate about photography, books, and music, and I almost never say no to a new adventure. You can call me Judy; my Vietnamese name is Dung, pronounced /zʊŋ/.

Selected Projects

  1. INFUSE: A Unified Surface Benchmark for Indirect Prompt Injection in Agentic Systems
    Thuy Dung Nguyen, Jingyi Guo , Rana Seda Dubauskas , and 3 more authors
    arXiv preprint arXiv:2610.20776, Oct 2026
    Abs PDF Code

    INFUSE is an end-to-end benchmark for indirect prompt injection (IPI) across long-horizon agent workflows. It plants adversarial instructions in the files, configs, file names, and archives a coding agent reads, then measures both whether the agent obeys and whether it still finishes the task. INFUSE covers 40 realistic tasks and 56 attack scenarios spanning five threat categories. Evaluating 19 LLMs over 41,824 adversarial trajectories, we find per-model attack success ranging from 3.2% to 41.9%, limited protection from existing guard models, and substantial, model-dependent security-utility trade-offs for defenses.

  2. SUGAR: A Sweeter Spot for Generative Unlearning of Many Identities
    Dung Thuy Nguyen, Quang Nguyen , Preston K. Robinette , and 3 more authors
    In 2026 IEEE/CVF Winter Conference on Applications of Computer Vision (WACV) , Mar 2026
    Abs PDF Code

    Recent advances in 3D-aware generative models have enabled high-fidelity image synthesis of human identities. However, this progress raises urgent questions around user consent and the ability to remove specific individuals from a model’s output space. We address this by introducing SUGAR, a framework for scalable generative unlearning that enables the removal of many identities (simultaneously or sequentially) without retraining the entire model. Rather than projecting unwanted identities to unrealistic outputs or relying on static template faces, SUGAR learns a personalized surrogate latent for each identity, diverting reconstructions to visually coherent alternatives while preserving the model’s quality and diversity. We further introduce a continual utility preservation objective that guards against degradation as more identities are forgotten. SUGAR achieves state-of-the-art performance in removing up to 200 identities, while delivering up to a 700% improvement in retention utility compared to existing baselines.

  3. PBP: Post-training Backdoor Purification for Malware Classifiers
    Dung Thuy Nguyen, Ngoc N Tran , Taylor T Johnson , and 1 more author
    Network and Distributed System Security Symposium (NDSS), 2025
    Abs PDF Code

    This research introduces PBP, a post-training defense for malware classifiers to mitigate backdoor poisoning attacks without relying on specific embedding mechanisms. By regulating batch normalization statistics to correct activation distribution distortions caused by backdoors, PBP effectively restores model performance to a clean state. Experiments show that PBP reduces attack success rates from 100% to nearly 0% using just 1% of training data, significantly outperforming state-of-the-art methods.

  4. Backdoor attacks and defenses in federated learning: Survey, challenges and future research directions
    Thuy Dung Nguyen, Tuan Nguyen , Phi Le Nguyen , and 3 more authors
    Engineering Applications of Artificial Intelligence, 2024
    Abs PDF

    Federated learning (FL) has emerged as a promising paradigm for training machine learning models on decentralized data. However, FL is vulnerable to backdoor attacks, where a malicious client injects a backdoor pattern into its local data to manipulate the global model. This paper provides a comprehensive survey of backdoor attacks and defenses in FL. We first introduce the background of FL and backdoor attacks, followed by a detailed review of existing backdoor attacks and defenses in FL. We then discuss the challenges and future research directions in this area. Finally, we provide a summary of the surveyed works and suggest potential research directions for future studies.

  5. Iba: Towards irreversible backdoor attacks in federated learning
    Thuy Dung Nguyen, Tuan A Nguyen , Anh Tran , and 2 more authors
    Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems, 2024
    Abs PDF Code

    We propose a novel backdoor attack framework in FL, the Irreversible Backdoor Attack (IBA), that jointly learns the optimal and visually stealthy trigger and then gradually implants the backdoor into a global model. This approach allows the adversary to execute a backdoor attack that can evade both human and machine inspections. Additionally, we enhance the efficiency and durability of the proposed attack by selectively poisoning the model’s parameters that are least likely updated by the main task’s learning process and constraining the poisoned model update to the vicinity of the global model.

  6. FedGrad: Mitigating Backdoor Attacks in Federated Learning Through Local Ultimate Gradients Inspection
    Thuy Dung Nguyen, Anh Duy Nguyen , Thanh-Hung Nguyen , and 4 more authors
    In 2023 International Joint Conference on Neural Networks (IJCNN) , 2023
    Abs PDF Code

    We propose FedGrad, a novel backdoor-resistant defense for FL that is resistant to cutting-edge backdoor attacks, including the edge-case attack, and performs effectively under heterogeneous client data and a large number of compromised clients. FedGrad is designed as a two-layer filtering mechanism that thoroughly analyzes the ultimate layer’s gradient to identify suspicious local updates and remove them from the aggregation process. We evaluate FedGrad under different attack scenarios and show that it significantly outperforms state-of-the-art defense mechanisms. Notably, FedGrad can almost 100% correctly detect the malicious participants, thus providing a significant reduction in the backdoor effect (e.g., backdoor accuracy is less than 8%) while not reducing the main accuracy on the primary task.

  7. Joint optimization of charging location and time for network lifetime extension in WRSNs
    Thuy Dung Nguyen, Tuyen Nguyen , Thanh Hung Nguyen , and 2 more authors
    IEEE Transactions on Green Communications and Networking, 2021
    Abs PDF

    Wireless rechargeable sensor networks (WRSNs) have emerged as a potential solution to solve the challenge of prolonging battery-powered sensor networks’ lifetime. In a WRSN, a mobile charger moves around and charges the rechargeable sensors when stopping at charging spots. This paper newly considers a joint optimization of the charging location and the charging time to avoid node failure in WRSNs. We formulate the optimization and propose a solution for that. The proposal includes an algorithm to find a list of potential charging locations and their associated optimal charging time. Moreover, the Q-learning technique is adopted to determine the optimal next charging location among the candidates. We implement the algorithm and conduct experiments to compare it to the related works. The results show that the proposed algorithm outperforms the others in terms of network lifetime. Specifically, the highest performance gap of our proposal to the best algorithm among the others is 8.29 times.
